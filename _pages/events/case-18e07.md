summary: >-
  A warlock of the infernal order of Asmodeus stood at the door of Xarlus
  Shale's cramped little efficiency. Small flames danced across his skin and
  over the fiery tattoos that covered his body. He pounded on the wood and
  demanded that someone open it. Above him, Ozborn Underfoot had crept into the
  ceiling of the hallway and clung there, trying not to sweat. The warlock could
  not see him. Inside the apartment, the rest of the party had already thrown
  open the window and were climbing out, because they had heard the bang on the
  door. Vondal had turned the key in the lock to buy them a few extra moments.
  Then he stepped away and vanished, and the key vanished with him.


  Behind the locked door, the party argued in whispers. They worried that the
  local law would take Xarlus away, and that any interrogation they needed would
  be lost. Bec reasoned that a crew of strangers who answered to nobody would
  tear the apartment apart and ruin the investigation. Precedent said the agents
  might outrank local law enforcement, but the answer depended on the
  circumstances, and nothing about this case was clear-cut. They agreed the
  safest course was to get their witness out of the room and out of reach of
  anyone they could not trust. Freki went through the window first and waited on
  the fire escape, ready to carry Xarlus across the alley on his back.


  The warlock's fury grew with every second. Ozborn, watching from above, saw
  that this was a man who lived in a constant state of simmering rage. Beneath
  it he sensed an eagerness, as if the man hoped nobody would answer so he could
  start breaking things. Bec pressed herself flat against the wall beside the
  door, where she would be on the attacking side if it burst open. With a snap
  of her fingers, a cane appeared in her hand out of nowhere. She crushed one of
  her crystals of subtlety and silently conjured a thick cloud of fog. It rolled
  out from her feet and started to creep under the door, with none of the sound
  that would have given her away. The warlock pounded a third time and shouted
  his final warning, promising dire consequences.


  Xarlus was stunned and slow, but he stumbled toward the window and clambered
  through, helped by a rush of adrenaline. Callie whispered urgently for him to
  hurry. She followed him out a moment later, her wings fluttering at just the
  right instant to keep her steady. Freki seized Xarlus around the middle and
  tried to heave him over his shoulder. Xarlus wriggled in confusion and slowed
  him down, so the grab held but the leap could not happen yet. Meanwhile Bec,
  still hidden in the fog, backed toward the window, determined not to lose the
  spell that kept the fog thick between her friends and the door.


  Ozborn did not wait for the door to break. He let go of the ceiling and
  dropped onto the warlock's back like a falling stone. He wrapped his arms
  around the man's horns and clamped his legs around his torso, as if riding a
  bull. He bellowed, "Xarlus Shale, you're coming with me!", even though he knew
  perfectly well that was not who he held. For a moment he had the man in his
  grip. Then the air exploded in a burst of brimstone and the warlock vanished,
  and Ozborn landed on his feet in a choking yellow cloud. He kept shouting into
  the smoke, trying to keep up the ruse that he had Xarlus.


  Out on the fire escape, Freki finally got Xarlus settled and sprang. He
  bunched his legs, braced against the rusty railing, and pushed off with the
  strength of a werewolf and the help of his ring. The railing buckled under him
  and sheared away, and the leap lost its force. He caught the far railing with
  both hands, and that one tore loose too. Metal screeched and then fell the
  full six stories to the alley. Freki dropped and slid down Xarlus's body until
  he was hanging on to the man's leg. Xarlus, white-faced, had somehow clamped
  one hand around the fire escape floor, and that one hand was all that held
  them both.


  Callie flew to them, using her hovering wings and her pixie dust. She caught
  Xarlus under the arms and flapped as hard as she could to take some of the
  weight from his straining fingers. Bec squeezed out of the window with the fog
  still bulging after her, and she took in how bad things looked. She called out
  a spell of feather fall and told them to trust her and let go. Freki released
  the man's leg and drifted slowly downward. He caught the fifth-floor landing
  and braced his legs, ready to spring back up. Xarlus began hauling himself up
  with Callie's help.


  The warlock then appeared on the sixth-floor landing, arms crossed and smug.
  He looked down on the circus below and said, "Hold on to him tightly. I'd hate
  for him to fall." When Xarlus reached up and begged for help, the warlock
  offered an arm to clasp. Callie let go. Xarlus rose toward the man who meant
  to take him. Freki saw it and sprang from the fifth-floor platform. He grabbed
  Xarlus and wrenched him free, and the force dragged the warlock off the
  railing as well. The three of them tumbled through the air together.


  Xarlus and Freki floated down under the feather-fall spell. The warlock had no
  such protection and plunged faster, still gripping Xarlus's arm and dragging
  at him like dead weight. The flames that licked across the warlock's skin
  burned Xarlus's wrist where he held it, and Xarlus cried out in pain. Callie
  saw the fire and realized it was not a deliberate attack. It was simply the
  nature of the man, and it was killing the witness. As she sank after them, she
  called on divine power and healed Xarlus's mangled hand and wrist. His
  injuries closed, but she felt how little life he had to give, and she knew a
  hard punch could put him in the hospital. Inside the building, Ozborn was
  still pounding on a neighbor's door, shouting that it was SBI business and an
  emergency.


  A shadow slipped silently through the apartment window and came to stand
  beside Bec. It was Vex, and the only reason she knew was that he had landed so
  close to her. In a single smooth motion a crossbow bolt shot from a concealed
  mechanism in his sleeve and struck the warlock in the arm. The jolt made Vael,
  the warlock, lose his grip, and he fell five stories and hit the ground hard.
  Vex turned to Bec with dead seriousness and told her to take him down before
  he could summon the others. Bec reached out with a crackling beam of lightning
  that linked her to the fallen man below.


  Vael rose to his feet while the lightning still ate at him, apparently
  unbothered. With a flick of one hand, an enormous cage of force sprang up
  around Freki and Xarlus as they drifted toward the street, and Callie, who had
  followed them down, was caught inside it too. Its bars were invisible, and
  they were half an inch thick and half an inch apart. Vael then released a
  roaring column of red-gold hellfire into the sky, a beacon that could be seen
  across the whole harbor district. "Now we wait for my brothers," he said. "Do
  enjoy the view, Agent." Xarlus lifted his hands and begged him to stop,
  claiming he surrendered. Vael only smirked and told him that his turn was
  next.


  Ozborn finally got a neighbor to open his door. The old man peered out over a
  security chain while Ozborn argued, pleaded, and threatened him. Through the
  gap, Ozborn saw an apartment crowded with carved wooden animals, the life's
  work of someone skilled and now past his prime. When the door finally opened,
  he rushed through and found the window shut. He did not slow down. He ran and
  leapt, daggers in hand, and burst through the glass. As he plunged down six
  stories, he hurled one dagger at the warlock below. Time seemed to slow as he
  threw, and the blade went straight into Vael's chest. Green flame leapt from
  the strike, jumped to the barbed devil who now stood near Vael, and burned
  them both. Ozborn teleported to the dagger the instant it struck and landed in
  the middle of the fray.


  In the cage, Freki leaned against the bars and loosed an arrow tipped with
  crystallized black lotus extract, meant to put a man to sleep. It hit Vael
  square in the chest. A tattoo on the warlock's forearm flared, and some foul
  magic took a piece of his essence and spared him the arrow's effect. Callie
  and Bec both felt it. His infernal pact had protected him, but at a heavy
  price, and he could not pay it forever. Freki's second arrow glanced off the
  fire that wrapped the man. Freki changed into his full wolf form and snarled
  in the cage. Vael, who had been briefly shaken, mocked him: "A dog in a cage.
  Very appropriate." But Freki smelled a faint trace of desperation beneath all
  that arrogance.


  Vael touched an amulet at his throat, and it glimmered with radiant light. A
  barbed devil, bristling with spines, appeared at his side. Freki and Callie
  understood that the devil had been placed between Vael and the direction Bec's
  lightning came from. Callie saw that it drank in some of the electrical energy
  meant for its master. Callie also grasped that the half-inch gaps in the cage
  were just wide enough for something very small. She hid behind Freki, who
  puffed himself up to block the warlock's sight. In a whisper of magic she
  turned into a mouse, slipped through the bars, and hid behind a pile of trash
  in the alley. Vael noticed that she was gone and demanded to know where she
  had gone, seemingly speaking to something invisible. Callie guessed he had an
  imp as a familiar, hovering unseen, and that this was how he had known where
  they were.


  Vael answered Ozborn's attack with his own. He raised both hands and fired
  beams of dark, agonizing energy at Bec on the fire escape. The tattoo on her
  skin cut the force of the blows. Even so, the last of them hurled her backward
  through the railing and off the edge of the platform. She caught the lip of
  the landing with one hand, but the blow had shattered her concentration, and
  her lightning died. Vael laughed. Xarlus begged him to stop fighting, but Vael
  told him it was no longer about him. When Bec tried to haul herself up, she
  slipped and fell. She had just enough time to cast feather fall on herself,
  and she drifted down to land on her feet in the alley.


  Callie turned Xarlus into a mouse as well. She called him over to the bars and
  told him gently what she could do. He was terrified, but he had stopped being
  surprised, and he stepped close. She touched his finger through the bars, and
  in the next moment a tiny mouse streaked through the cage and ran away into
  the alley. Freki called on his ravens, Hugin and Munin, to find the warlock
  who had vanished into the brimstone. Munin reported that Vael had stepped
  through space but was still near. Hugin spoke only of "the one who comes." Vex
  then stepped out of the shadow, as if he had never been hidden, and looked
  straight at an empty spot at the corner of the cage. "You're alone, initiate,"
  he said. "Nobody sent you. Nobody's coming."


  Vael appeared in the place where Vex was looking, and he stared back at him.
  "Where is the civilian?" he demanded. "Tell me now." "In my pocket," said Vex.
  Vael told him to hand it over. As if his own will had been taken from him, Vex
  reached into his pocket and pulled out the mouse by its tail. Then a booming
  voice rolled across the alley. A grizzled, bearded warlock had arrived, and he
  stroked his chin as he looked over the party. "Initiate Vael," he said. "I'll
  be taking that, I think." He turned to the others and said coldly that they
  would discuss their trespass afterward.
