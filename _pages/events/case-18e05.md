summary: >-
  The night had grown heavy and watchful over the rough streets of Trell when a
  shadowy figure stepped out of a nearby alley, his smirk catching the dim light
  as he approached the party. He introduced himself through Taelin's startled
  recognition — Galloc, one of Kerwin's personal bodyguards — and offered a
  casual warning about making too much noise in a neighborhood like this. The
  sharpest eyes among the group caught the subtle flicker of his gaze toward
  Taelin, a momentary tell that betrayed a hidden interest beneath his easy,
  friendly manner. He was sizing them up, probing for weaknesses, and almost
  certainly angling for some kind of profit.


  The party played it cool, spinning a convincing story about heading to dinner
  and assuring Galloc that Kerwin was probably just working late. Galloc, for
  his part, was disarmingly candid about his own role — he was paid to watch the
  streets, not to babysit his employer inside a building. He hinted at working
  in the gray spaces between what governments could legally do and what they
  actually needed done, a confession that landed with knowing nods from the more
  worldly members of the group. Taelin stood rigid throughout the exchange,
  visibly tense and distrustful, though not quite terrified of the man she
  clearly knew from her years at the firm.


  When Galloc finally retreated back into the shadows of the alley, the group
  exhaled and pressed onward toward dinner. It was only a few blocks later that
  Taelin's hand flew to her side and her face went pale — her pouch was gone.
  She insisted it contained everything important to her, and the party
  immediately suspected Galloc, the only stranger they had encountered close
  enough to have taken it. But before they could form a proper plan, Taelin
  crumpled to her knees, clutching her head as a splitting headache overtook
  her. Those with a healer's eye recognized what was happening: the magical
  buffer that had been wrapped around her was finally, visibly dissolving away.


  Vondal, who had been quietly bottling up the knowledge of what Taelin had done
  that evening, could contain himself no longer. He blurted out the question
  that had been burning in him all night — was she freaking out because she had
  murdered a man? The words hung in the air, blunt and unavoidable. Taelin
  didn't deny it. Ozborn, ever the pragmatist, knelt beside her and offered the
  only comfort he knew how to give, remarking with genuine sincerity that he
  remembered his own first stabbing and that they could talk about it. It was,
  in its own strange way, the kindest thing anyone had said to her all evening.


  The group made a swift decision to split up. Callie and Bec would escort
  Taelin back to the SPI's local headquarters, keeping her safe inside a
  shimmering protective dome that rendered them invisible to the outside world.
  Freki and Ozborn would double back to track down Galloc and recover the stolen
  pouch. Taelin, still shaking, reluctantly climbed aboard the magical flying
  carpet and was ushered toward safety, the translucent barrier of the dome
  glowing softly around her as she stared out at the city she could no longer
  trust.


  Inside the dome, Taelin began to speak more freely. She described her
  condition in precise legal terms — constructive coercion by a hazardous
  artifact — and explained that the magical coffee pot had been warping the
  minds of everyone in Kerwin's orbit for years. She had known something was
  wrong, had even taken precautions, but the gas the carafe emitted had a way of
  making everything feel perfectly reasonable until you were far enough away
  from it to remember who you used to be. She had once had a partner, she said,
  and a life outside the firm, but she could no longer even recall her partner's
  name.


  Meanwhile, Freki dropped to a crouch in the alley where Galloc had stood and
  breathed in the lingering traces of the man's scent — brimstone and the
  outdoors, the unmistakable signature of a tiefling who spent more time in
  alleys than drawing rooms. There was a second scent too, fainter and older,
  suggesting someone else had been present before Galloc emerged. His raven,
  Huginn, appeared on a neighboring rooftop and signaled with a tilt of his
  head, and the chase was on. Freki and Ozborn leaped and teleported their way
  across the rooftops of Trell, following the raven's guidance from building to
  building until they arrived at a flat roof featuring a small community garden.


  Ozborn immediately helped himself to a tomato, eating it like a piece of hand
  fruit while Freki investigated the garden with considerably more focus. The
  scent trail ended near the tomato plants, and after careful searching, Freki
  discovered a cleverly concealed trapdoor hidden beneath the disturbed dust of
  the rooftop. Wary of traps, the two backed away and sent Ozborn's flying
  monkey, Churro, to open it. Churro scratched at it, pawed it, and jumped on it
  in mounting frustration until the door banged open on one of his leaps — and a
  hand reached up from below.


  Galloc climbed out of the trapdoor looking considerably less amused than he
  had on the street. He acknowledged that they had gone to impressive lengths to
  follow him, and the two agents launched into a layered conversation — Ozborn
  speaking aloud while simultaneously communicating in Thieves' Cant beneath the
  surface, pressing Galloc about the stolen bag. Galloc denied taking anything,
  but his eyes said otherwise. Freki let his own eyes shift to a cold, predatory
  yellow, and Galloc took one smooth step back, putting the trapdoor between
  them.


  While Ozborn kept Galloc talking, Freki turned his attention to the tomato
  plants and began to commune with them in the quiet, whispering language of
  growing things. The plant he spoke with — a cheerful, chatty specimen that
  introduced itself as Potato — turned out to be a surprisingly reliable
  witness. Potato confirmed that Galloc visited often, sometimes covered in
  blood, and always smoking near the leaves in a way that clogged up its little
  pores. But there was also a woman who came to the roof: a tiefling with a tail
  who sang to the plants, sometimes disappeared without warning, and was, in
  Potato's enthusiastic opinion, far better company than Galloc had ever been.
  Freki promised to bring food, sang a brief song to encourage growth, and bid
  farewell to his new tomato child, departing as Agent Papa.


  The intelligence pointed clearly toward a second bodyguard — a woman named
  Preen, who matched the description of someone who could vanish at will and who
  had likely been the one to lift Taelin's bag while Galloc held the party's
  attention on the street. With that piece in place, Ozborn laid out a deal for
  Galloc plainly: the SPI would ensure the case never went to court and the
  carafe would leave town, and in exchange, Galloc would point them toward the
  artifact's current location. Galloc agreed, hinting that something would turn
  up soon if the agents kept their word and left his crew alone. He dropped back
  through the trapdoor, and the two agents made their way down from the rooftops
  — Freki via a rusted fire escape, Ozborn by dropping a dagger to the street
  below and appearing beside it.


  The triumphant return to the local SPI office was immediately humbled by a
  locked door. Vex had gone home for the night and taken the only key with him,
  leaving behind nothing but an IOU note promising a visit to the locksmith.
  What followed was a prolonged and increasingly chaotic series of attempts to
  gain entry: Vondal's familiar Murphy, in cat form, managed to pop a window
  latch and get inside, but the door remained stubbornly shut. Vondal attempted
  to manipulate the lock's internal mechanisms with a spectral hand,
  accidentally gumming up the tumblers with ectoplasm. By the time Freki and
  Ozborn arrived to find their teammates still locked out, Ozborn's attempt with
  his thieves' tools ended in failure and the eventual, disgusted disposal of
  the entire set into a nearby dumpster.


  The party eventually coordinated a solution involving the portable hole — a
  magical extradimensional space furnished with tiki torches and a folding table
  — to get everyone inside without further damaging the property. Freki, who
  harbored a deep and well-documented trauma around the portable hole from a
  previous incident, grumbled through the entire process. Once inside, they used
  the office's teleportation circle to travel back to the main SPI headquarters,
  where Mallory was waiting and the lights in Cassandra's lab were still
  burning.


  Bec and Ozborn briefed Mallory on the situation with the blunt efficiency of
  agents who had seen stranger things: there had been a murder, they had the
  suspect in custody, and she had formally requested asylum. Mallory raised an
  eyebrow at the detail that the murder suspect had been left alone in a
  portable hole with one of his best agents, but the party assured him that
  Callie was perfectly capable of handling herself. Cassandra was pulled from
  her lab, looking haggard from a long night of paperwork, and the group
  gathered in Mallory's office to conduct a proper interview.


  Taelin, now seated and somewhat steadier, laid out the full story with the
  precision of someone who had spent years managing legal documents. She had
  been with Kerwin's firm for three years, and the carafe's influence had been
  present the entire time, slowly eroding her memories of her previous life, her
  partner, her interests, everything that had made her herself. Six weeks ago, a
  business trip to Abriymoch on the judicial island of Phlegethos had taken her
  far enough from the artifact that her mind had begun to clear. She had used
  that clarity to write herself a letter, mailed it home, and returned to the
  firm knowing she would fall back under the influence — but trusting her
  clear-headed self to have left her a way out.


  The letter had contained a plan to steal the carafe and break Kerwin's hold on
  the firm. When Kerwin refused to give it up, Taelin's anger had overtaken her,
  and the plan had ended in violence. She described the legal defense she had
  researched — constructive coercion by a hazardous artifact — and noted that
  the lawyer Sorelda Voth's team had independently uncovered the same ancient
  precedent and was likely planning to use it to pursue Kerwin's estate. The
  carafe, she insisted, could not be allowed to remain in circulation. It had
  already destroyed too many lives.


  Cassandra stepped forward and attempted to perform a miracle to clear the
  remaining corruption from Taelin's mind, her eyes blazing white as she uttered
  a word of incomprehensible power — but the effort left her trembling and
  spent. She had pushed herself too hard for too long. Rather than give up, she
  turned to Callie and offered what remained of her strength, merging her
  essence temporarily with the gnome cleric's so that Callie could channel the
  power of not two but three divine presences at once. Callie's medallion burned
  hot against her chest as she laid her hands on Taelin's head and called upon
  her goddesses with everything she had.


  The flash of energy that followed was clean and complete. Taelin's eyes
  snapped open, sharp and clear, and she said simply that she thought it had
  worked. The three years of slow, grinding magical corruption dissolved in an
  instant, and for the first time in a very long time, Taelin was entirely
  herself again. She remembered the name of her partner — Davan — and the place
  she had once been offered a job, Crestfall, and all the things she had given
  up without ever quite realizing she was giving them up.


  With Taelin restored and secured, Ozborn pulled Mallory aside for a quiet
  word. He explained that he had made contact with a figure in the local
  underworld who had agreed to provide a tip on the carafe's location, and that
  the criminal elements in Trell wanted the artifact gone just as badly as the
  SPI did. He requested discretionary funds to pursue the lead through the
  city's underworld channels, and Mallory granted it without hesitation. Ozborn
  looked at the bag of coin, looked at the rest of his team, and announced with
  complete confidence that he had a plan — it was a good plan, it was going to
  cost some money, and they were going to get the receipts.
