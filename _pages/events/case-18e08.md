summary: >-
  The confrontation in the alley took a sudden and dramatic turn when a
  grizzled, scarred warlock arrived to interrupt the standoff between the party
  and the young hothead [Vael](../dossiers/vael). The elder man exuded a calm, iron authority that
  was entirely unlike the volatile energy of the youth he had come to collect,
  and he announced himself with a simple, booming demand: he would be taking the
  mouse. Before he could finish addressing the [SPI](../orgs/spi) agents about their trespass,
  however, the world itself seemed to buckle. Time stretched and warped around
  them, the clear morning sky darkened without warning, and unnatural snow began
  to drift down from clouds that had not been there a moment before. From no
  particular direction, a deep and ululating throat singing filled the air, and
  [Vondal](../dossiers/vondal) appeared in the alley as if he had always been there, brushing against
  a shimmering, invisible cage that held [Freki](../dossiers/freki) within it.


  The elder warlock's arrival had shattered something in Vael. Where the young
  man had been furious before, he was now beyond reason — livid, desperate, and
  afraid in equal measure. Those among the party with a keen eye for the
  supernatural noticed that the elder warlock had done something subtle and
  devastating: with a single, surreptitious gesture, he had placed a mark upon
  Vael, a supernatural binding that linked his own consciousness to the young
  warlock's. Freki, whose instincts as a hunter ran deep, felt the nature of it
  immediately — it was the relationship of a watchful parent to a wayward child,
  a predator who had no need to chase because the prey could never truly run.
  Vael could not teleport away. He could not hide. He was caught, and he knew
  it.


  What Vael did next was the act of a cornered animal. He ripped the sleeve from
  his arm to reveal blood-red tattooed incantations written in tiny, dense
  script, and he screamed his invocation to the heavens: "My Lord [Asmodeus](../dossiers/asmodeus), I
  invoke you. I beseech you, come to my aid." The alley erupted. Vael burst into
  living flame, his feet left the ground, and his eyes became twin points of
  fire as an infernal crown materialized above his head. The presence that
  manifested around him was not Vael's — it was something ancient and immense,
  something that made the young warlock feel ten feet tall even as it dwarfed
  him entirely. A wave of supernatural terror and submission crashed over the
  party, and those who could not withstand it felt an overwhelming compulsion to
  kneel before the King of the Nine, Asmodeus, Lord of [Trell](../locales/trell), who had arrived.


  The elder warlock and [Vex](../dossiers/vex-morradine) froze mid-motion, caught in the amber of stopped
  time, while the snow hung suspended in the air around them. The party, by
  virtue of something fundamental in their natures, remained free to move and
  speak within the bubble of frozen reality. Asmodeus, his voice carrying the
  weight of ages, addressed them with a terrible courtesy. He told them he had
  been summoned by Vael, who had offered his soul in exchange for the devil's
  momentary attention, but that his interest in lingering was their own doing —
  specifically, the blessings they carried, some of which he would rather not
  have in his lands. He invited them to call him Lord, and when [Bec](../dossiers/bec) cautiously
  did so, she felt something metaphysical slip from her grasp like a stone
  dropped from a great height — before the [Celestial Hand](../relics/celestial-hand) around her neck caught
  it, steadying her, reminding her that Asmodeus was not her lord.


  [Ozborn](../dossiers/oz), terrified and utterly incapable of walking away from something this
  interesting, was the first to engage. He nervously addressed the archdevil,
  and in his stumbling attempt to find the right words, he called him "my
  personal savior" — a slip that felt far more earnest than intended, and that
  settled around his soul like a quiet promise. Asmodeus found the halfling's
  subsequent attempt to charm him merely cute, but he was not without interest.
  He offered Ozborn his patronage and a suitable replacement for his daggers,
  and when Ozborn declared that he always wished for more, the agreement formed
  around him before he fully understood what he had agreed to. He handed over
  his linked magical daggers, and in the same moment, a wellspring of psionic
  power surged to life within him — the daggers had always been an expression of
  something that was already his. He could feel the psychic blades waiting to be
  called forth from his own mind.


  While Ozborn was making his bargain, the rest of the party was attempting to
  leave. Vondal pushed toward the edge of the alley and discovered that the
  temporal bubble had a boundary — the air grew thick and viscous the further he
  went, like pressing through the skin of a soap bubble. He paused there, and
  [Callie](../dossiers/callie) joined him, and together they began urging Bec to follow. But Bec
  hesitated. She knew, with the certainty of a scholar who has spent a lifetime
  chasing knowledge, that the being in that alley possessed secrets she would
  never find on her own. The temptation was not the devil's power or his
  promises — it was the knowledge itself, the kind that sits just out of reach
  for an entire lifetime. Ozborn, newly flush with infernal energy and deeply
  convinced that the party should take every advantage on offer, rolled a
  natural surge of supernatural charm over Bec, and for a moment she was
  entirely certain that he was the wisest person she had ever met and that she
  should absolutely listen to him.


  It was Callie who broke the spell, throwing her weight against Bec and shoving
  her toward the edge of the bubble, with Freki moving in to help. But Bec's
  mind was faster than her body. Even as she was being physically pushed out of
  the frozen alley, she spoke her terms clearly and without hesitation: she was
  only interested in one exchange — the Celestial Hand for the secret that had
  been stolen from her. Asmodeus accepted. The deal was done in the space
  between one heartbeat and the next, and Bec was shoved out into the street
  with the knowledge of [Karach](../relics/karach) blooming back into her memory like a light being
  switched on — a rare, rainbow-hued substance that grew in deep caverns near
  the [Flux](../locales/flux) and acted as a natural antidote to its corrupting influence. She had
  traded a powerful artifact for a truth she had once known and lost, and she
  felt, with some complicated mixture of relief and unease, that it had been
  worth it.


  Ozborn, meanwhile, had not been idle. The moment after completing his deal
  with Asmodeus, he used the cover of a congratulatory high-five to pickpocket
  one of his surrendered daggers back from the archdevil's person. He succeeded.
  For approximately three seconds, he was the halfling who had stolen from the
  Lord of Trell. Then Asmodeus paralyzed him where he stood, and the
  alley went very quiet. The devil regarded him for a long moment before
  speaking with a terrifying, almost paternal calm: "I will forgive your
  indiscretion. I will allow you to hold that dagger. I will come to retrieve it
  eventually." He released Ozborn, and the halfling ran.


  As Ozborn sprinted past his companions shouting that everything was fine, time
  resumed its normal flow. The snow began to fall again. The throat singing
  faded, its last note hanging in the air before dissolving entirely. And in the
  alley behind them, the young warlock Vael collapsed to the ground and crumbled
  rapidly to dust, his soul claimed as payment for the manifestation he had
  demanded. The elder warlock blinked back into awareness, his face cycling
  through confusion and astonishment as he took in the shifted positions of
  everyone around him and the pile of ash that had been his subordinate.


  The grizzled warlock introduced himself as [Haskel Grieve](../dossiers/haskel-grieve), a Warlock of
  [Mammon](../dossiers/mammon), and he wasted no time asserting that what had just occurred fell
  squarely within his jurisdiction. He proposed cooperation with the SPI, and
  then, with a look of genuine unease, he noted that his ledger contained words
  he had not written — the frozen moment had somehow recorded itself in his
  magical notebook without his knowledge or consent. [Xarlus](../dossiers/xarlus-shale) emerged from behind
  a nearby dumpster, wide-eyed and shaken, having witnessed Vael's
  disintegration from a safe distance.


  Freki, meanwhile, was having a quieter and more unsettling realization. His
  ravens, [Hugin](../dossiers/hugin) and [Munin](../dossiers/munin), had been perched on his shoulders throughout the
  encounter, their protective presence a steady comfort. But there had been a
  third raven. He had felt it during the manifestation, watching from the fire
  escape above — and now, as if in answer to the question forming in his mind, a
  raven dropped from that same fire escape and landed in the street before them.
  It transformed. The orc shaman [Andalla](../dossiers/andalla) stood before the party, her gaze
  sweeping over them with sharp, assessing eyes, and she asked the only question
  that mattered: "What did he take from you?" Freki's jaw tightened. He
  remembered her. He remembered the centaur she had put back together after he
  had cut it in half, and he remembered that she had been watching the entire
  time — watching, and waiting, and now demanding answers from people who were
  still processing what had just happened to them.


  The party stood in the street outside the alley, battered and transformed in
  ways both visible and invisible. Ozborn had new power humming in his mind and
  a devil's promise of a future debt hanging over him. Bec had traded away a
  celestial artifact and reclaimed a secret that could change the course of
  their work entirely. Freki had three ravens where there should have been two.
  And somewhere in the Nine, a young warlock's soul had just been filed away.
  Haskel Grieve stood with his notebook open, Andalla stood with her question
  unanswered, and the snow continued to fall on all of them, quiet and
  relentless, as if the sky itself was keeping score.
